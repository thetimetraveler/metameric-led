What the lamp is doing

Both halves have exactly the same colour of white, so the walls match. Side B gets there with a different recipe of the same five LED channels. Paint reflects only the wavelengths it is given, so a different recipe makes some paints richer and others duller.

LED recipe, share of the light

AB

Five channels and one fixed white leave only two free dials. That is why vibrancy picks a direction (reds and magentas, greens and teals, yellows and blues) rather than a single colour. The 450 nm blue channel barely moves, because it alone sets the blue content of the white.

What happens to each paint

Chroma is how colourful a paint looks (CIELAB C*), measured against the shared white. The bar shows how side B compares with side A.

Model: CIE 1931 2° observer (Wyman et al. multi-lobe fit), a blackbody white at the chosen CCT, and five Gaussian LED channels at 450, 510, 570, 610 and 650 nm (σ = 9 nm). Paints are smooth, illustrative reflectance curves, not measured pigments. Side A is the LED recipe closest to natural light at that CCT. Side B moves along the two directions that leave the white unchanged. Eye adaptation partly rebalances colours toward daylight, as your eye does. The 3D room uses plain diffuse shading; every colour comes from the spectral maths. This is a teaching model, not a measurement of the strip in the build.
